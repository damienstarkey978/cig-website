In practice

CIG does not replace dispatch judgment or customer relationships.

CIG can build automations for order or load intake, email classification, task routing, document extraction, and exception notifications. An AI employee or agent can summarize approved operational information, prepare customer-update drafts, and direct unusual cases to a human. A dashboard can bring together job status, aging work, exceptions, response queues, and workload indicators so dispatch and leadership are looking at the same picture.

CIG does not replace dispatch judgment or customer relationships. It reduces the repetitive coordination surrounding them. The practical outcome is less time spent searching across systems, a clearer queue of work, and better consistency in updates and escalation. Implementation is shaped around the tools and handoffs already in use. Teams can start with one source of friction, such as status-request emails or exception follow-up, then validate the routing and dashboard before adding more data sources. Clear ownership and escalation rules keep automation useful instead of creating another inbox. The first version can focus on visibility and handoffs, giving dispatchers a clear place to confirm status or take over an unusual case. That keeps automation accountable when a shipment, customer, or carrier does not follow the normal path.
