In practice

CIG builds practical systems around those handoffs.

CIG can connect lead and estimate intake to the right team, organize project documents, summarize updates, route change-request information, and create reminders for approvals or missing details. An AI employee or agent can help draft internal summaries and prepare next-step tasks while leaving pricing, contract, safety, and project decisions with the responsible people. Dashboards can give owners and project managers a shared view of workload, status, exceptions, and pending actions.

The outcome is not a promise of effortless projects. It is less avoidable chasing, clearer accountability, and faster access to the information teams already have. CIG starts with one workflow that causes real friction, then expands only when the process is understood and useful. That first workflow might be estimate follow-up, change-order tracking, or daily update collection. The system can be documented so office staff and project leaders understand what it does, what it does not do, and when a person must take over. This approach also makes it easier to adjust the process when the company’s crews, software, or approval responsibilities change. It gives supervisors a shared operating picture without asking field teams to learn an unnecessary replacement system.
