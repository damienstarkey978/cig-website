In practice

CIG maps the workflow first.

Many teams do not need another app. They need fewer repetitive handoffs, better visibility into work, and a reliable way to turn incoming information into the next action. CIG maps the workflow first. That may mean connecting a form to a CRM, turning email and documents into structured tasks, giving staff an approved internal knowledge assistant, or building a dashboard that shows owners where work is waiting.

CIG serves Jacksonville-area and Northeast Florida businesses, including insurance agencies, construction companies, logistics operators, law firms, and accounting practices. Each industry has different information, approvals, deadlines, and risk points. A useful system respects those differences. It can keep people in control while handling the burnout work around them: sorting, summarizing, routing, checking, reminding, and reporting.

CIG provides custom AI automation consulting and implementation for businesses that want a right-sized system. That can include discovery, workflow design, integrations, prompt and agent design, dashboard development, testing, documentation, and a handoff plan. The exact scope depends on the workflow and systems involved, so CIG starts with an intake rather than publishing invented package prices.

A practical engagement may start with one measurable bottleneck: an overloaded inbox, a slow intake process, a report assembled by hand, or a manager who cannot see the current queue. CIG can map the inputs, decisions, system actions, and human approvals before recommending what to automate. This keeps the work grounded in an operating need instead of adding AI because it is fashionable.

For teams in Northeast Florida, the goal is a system people can actually use. That means clear ownership, sensible exception handling, and documentation for the staff who will live with the workflow. CIG can help identify a first build, connect it to existing tools where practical, and leave room to improve it as the business learns.
